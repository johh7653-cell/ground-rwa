# The GROUND thesis

## Your wallet. A real-world side.

A wallet can hold a token without explaining the world behind it. The symbol might refer to a company, a fund, vaulted metal, a property interest, a credit instrument or a collectible. Those relationships deserve more than a price tile.

GROUND brings the product, issuer, source trail and market reference into one place. Its role is to help people understand what they are looking at and make their own research plan explicit.

## From a symbol to a source

The complete library contains 1,936 asset records from 50 issuers across 16 categories. It starts with Solana's 1,042 primary quote-network records and keeps the wider catalogue available. A supported deployment and the network of a particular price observation are recorded separately.

An underlying asset, a token's legal/product structure and its secondary market answer different questions. GROUND keeps issuer descriptions and external source links attached to each entry. A catalogue listing alone establishes neither product eligibility nor ownership of the underlying asset.

## A plan belongs to its builder

Blueprint starts with a sample budget and category weights. Basket adds specific products from the complete library. Both make the user's chosen allocations visible and save validated planning records on their device.

Comparisons use saved reference prices or exact archived probe samples. Missing data remains missing. No assumed performance, yield or return is added to a user's plan.

## Connect context before execution

A compatible Solana wallet can connect its public account, and GROUND can read its mainnet SOL balance. Trade adds asset selection, a sample USDC amount and independently fetched market references. Connection does not reserve funds or sign an order.

Actual buying is a future integration. A live trading service will need executable routes, product-specific token units, explicit fees, simulation and a wallet-approved transaction. The current application does not claim these exist.

## One name, separate meanings

GROUND is the project's identity. Third-party assets retain their own issuers, backing and product terms. A future GROUND community token, if configured by the owner, is separate from those products; the current project has no configured contract address or token launch terms.

## A useful starting point

The first milestone is already tangible: a runnable research workspace with a complete source archive, real market-reference requests, public-account connection and practical local tools. Further work builds on those observable capabilities.
